     What to do
     ──────────

     Consume it: ? to propagate, match to handle both cases, .withDefault(...)
     to supply one — or, when you really mean to drop it, the explicit and
     greppable .ignore().

     Why
     ───

     There are two places you can throw a Result away and no third: bound to a _
     in a let, or left standing as an expression statement. Both are this error,
     so must-use is total rather than a convention. .ignore() is then the single
     spelling of a deliberate drop, which buri lint reports as discarded-result.

     The compiler looks for the _ anywhere in the pattern, not only at its head,
     so let (count, _) = (1, mayFail()); is this error too.
